For FMCG brands selling on 10-minute delivery apps

Find out which dark-store rupee actually sells.

DarkAisle reads your quick-commerce sales, stock and promo data and tells you, store by store, which promos made money after pantry loading and cannibalisation, where stock-outs are leaking sales, and which listings to add or drop. Then it splits next month's trade budget for you.

Demo: fictional brand "Chatkara Foods", 10 SKUs, 18 dark stores in 3 cities, 3 generic platforms, 12 weeks of seeded synthetic data.

The problem

Promo reports flatter you

Platform dashboards show promo-week sales against last week. They leave out the dip after the promo, when shoppers have stocked up, and the sales your other SKUs lost to it. A promo can look like +80% and still lose money.

Stock-outs are invisible

A dark store holds a few thousand SKUs on a few shelves. When your bestseller runs out at 7pm on a Saturday, the shopper buys a rival and you never see that sale in any report.

Range is decided store by store

Each dark store lists only part of your range. Brand teams rarely know which stores are missing a SKU that sells well in the store down the road, or which listings just take up shelf space.

How it works

  1. Connect the platform sales and inventory exports you already get (CSV, or an API where the platform offers one).
  2. Baseline: for every store and SKU, DarkAisle estimates what would have sold with no promo and full stock, adjusting for day of the week and using only clean days nearby.
  3. Read the promo honestly: incremental units minus the post-promo dip and same-category cannibalisation, priced at your real margin and funding share.
  4. Act: a ranked list of promos to scale, rework or stop (with the best discount depth), stock leaks to fix, listings to pitch, and a budget split with a payback hurdle.

Live demo synthetic data

Each promo ran on one platform for 7 days. "Naive uplift" compares with the week before, which is what most dashboards show. "True uplift" compares with a day-of-week adjusted, stock-adjusted baseline. Profit is net of the 7-day post-promo dip, cannibalisation of same-category SKUs, discount cost (at the brand's funding share) and the platform fee.

Actions

Velocity = units per fully stocked, non-promo day (weekday adjusted). Gaps are SKUs not listed here that sell in at least two same-city stores, scaled by this store's size index. Tail = less than half the store's median margin per day and well below same-city peers.

Each lever has a diminishing-returns curve: cap × (1 − 2−spend / half-spend). Caps for availability, promos and listings come from the analysis; the sponsored-ads lever is an editable assumption. Money goes to whichever lever returns the most for the next ₹5,000, and stops when the next step falls below the hurdle.

Startup brief

  

    
Target customer
Brand, e-commerce and trade-marketing managers at emerging and mid-size FMCG brands (snacks, beverages, personal care) that get 15–40% of online sales from quick commerce in India, and Australian challenger brands entering rapid-delivery apps. Beachhead: Indian D2C-born food and beverage brands with ₹20–200 crore revenue.

    
Positioning
For FMCG brand teams who spend trade money on quick-commerce apps without knowing what it returns, DarkAisle is the store-level profit lens that shows what each promo, stock fix and listing really earns. Unlike platform dashboards, it counts the dip and cannibalisation and puts a rupee value on stock-outs.

    
Go-to-market
  • Free "promo post-mortem": upload one promo's export, get a one-page readout (lead magnet).
  • LinkedIn content on promo myths for FMCG and D2C operators; founder-led outbound to heads of e-commerce.
  • Partnerships with quick-commerce agencies and distributors who already run brands' accounts.
  • Land with promo ROI, expand to availability alerts and range planning.

    
Key metrics
  • North star: incremental margin found per customer per month
  • Pilot → paid conversion (target 35%)
  • Time to first readout (target < 48 hours)
  • Weekly active brand users; alert action rate
  • Net revenue retention (target 115%+)
